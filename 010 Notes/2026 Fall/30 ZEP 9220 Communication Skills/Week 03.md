## Summary

### 🚨 I. Core Crisis Communication Principles
*   **Speed and Transparency:** Respond quickly ("Tell it fast") by communicating everything ("Tell it all"). The goal is to regain control immediately and guide the narrative toward resolution.
*   **Proactive Action:** Always inform stakeholders of the steps being taken to resolve the crisis, and when the issue will be over.
*   **Reputation Management:** Reputation is defined by a combination of **Performance + Behavior + Communications**.

### ❌ II. What NOT To Do (The Don'ts)
Avoid behaviors that undermine trust or escalate panic:
*   Lying, telling misleading half-truths, or only sharing partial stories.
*   Denying the severity of the problem or assigning blame.
*   Panicking or "shooting the messenger."

### ✅ III. Best Practices (The Dos)
Focus on immediate and controlled communication:
*   Be fully transparent ("Tell it all").
*   Immediately communicate your planned corrective actions ("Tell them what you're doing to do about it").
*   Maintain a single, unified voice for the organization.

### ⏳ IV. The Crisis News Cycle (Timeline Strategy)
The window of opportunity and control diminishes rapidly:
*   **Golden Hour (First 45 Minutes):** Maximum influence. Use holding statements ("we're investigating...") to manage initial rumors.
*   **6 Hours:** Control is slipping; focus on damage management and getting your side of the story out.
*   **3 Days:** Sustained, visible reputational damage. The narrative can become self-perpetuating through continuous coverage.
*   **2 Weeks:** Rumors are often accepted as fact ("perception becomes reality").

### 🎤 V. Personal Communication Skills (Handling Pressure)
When speaking publicly:
*   Practice **Bridging**: If you don't know the answer, do not panic. Bridge back to your key messages and stick to a prepared script.
*   **Anticipate Failures:** Learn from failures ("train wrecks") as they reinforce what is true.

### 🏥 VI. Strategic Application (Case Study Focus)
In high-stakes scenarios (like hospital crises), communication must address:
*   **Decision Making:** Analyze all options (e.g., paying a ransom) by detailing the advantages, disadvantages, and public perception of each choice.
*   **Prioritization:** Define clear protocols for critical resources and patients.
*   **Comprehensive Planning:** Develop detailed communications plans that account for worst-case scenarios and reach all affected parties (patients, families, staff).

### 🎯 **Overall Goal & Stakes**
*   **Objective:** Present actionable recommendations in response to an immediate crisis.
*   **Stakes:** The outcome is existential—the company faces either closure or acquisition ("If this goes south...").
*   **Tone/Context:** The presentation must acknowledge the high urgency and short notice (e.g., starting with a phrase like, "Thank you for coming here at a late hour...").

### 📊 **Structure & Constraints**
*   **Length:** Approximately 6–7 slides.
*   **Pacing Warning:** Be extremely concise; avoid excessive bullet points to prevent exceeding the time limit and losing the audience's attention.
*   **Deliverable Timeline:** Draft is due Nov 21 (Week 6).

### 💡 **Required Content Focus Areas**

**1. Situation Analysis (The Opening)**
*   Dedicate a slide to assessing the current situation, emphasizing that **reputation risk** is the greatest vulnerability facing the hospital.

**2. The Three Core Recommendation Buckets (CEO's Pain Points)**
The presentation must provide clear answers and recommendations for these three areas:
*   **Ransom Strategy:** What actions should be taken regarding the ransom payment/situation?
*   **Operations & Profit Centers:** Reviewing current hospital operations, identifying profit centers, and providing concrete recommendations for improvement.
*   **Communications (Comms):** Developing a clear communication strategy by defining key audiences and offering guidance (no sample copy needed).

### Crisis News Cycle
- Pay attention to rumors
- Work to eliminate them as quickly as possible - within the first 45 minutes
- Best approach: dispel them or don't comment (litigation)
	- Dispel them by saying why it's not true (may need evidence)
	- don't comment may imply guilty
	- Is this something we can shut down or is this nothing or if we ignore it, will it go away?  If we say something, are we making it legitimate

- 45 minutes (also called the golden hour, 60 minutes is the most)
	- You have maximum influence over the outcome 
	- Can use a holding statement, "we're investigating..."
- 6 hours
	- Once a story crosses a wire service, is broadcast, or becomes subject of social media, it is out of your control
	- can no longer control the story
	- all you can do is manage the damage and get our side of the story out
	- can still be managed, but at a reputational cost
- 3 Days
	- Once printed by newspapers, expect story to be alive for several days
	- Sustained reputational damage
	- Can be near fatal if not stopped or addressed
	- Can expect 2 weeks of negative coverage 
	- The only thing to stop this is when the public gets tired of it
	- Print media starts breaking new angles and can give it new life and broadcast media may pick the story back up
	- cycle becomes self perpetuating 
- 2 Weeks
	- print media focus
	- expect continuous coverage until it runs out or another story takes over
	- rumors accepted as fact, perception becomes reality

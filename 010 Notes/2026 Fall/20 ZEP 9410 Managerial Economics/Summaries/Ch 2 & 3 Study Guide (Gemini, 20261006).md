## Chapter 2: Market Forces: Demand and Supply

### 1. Core Concepts & Definitions

- **Market:** A institution or mechanism that brings together buyers and sellers of a good or service.
    
- **Demand Curve:** Illustrates the relationship between the price of a good and the quantity consumers are willing and able to purchase, holding all other factors constant (Law of Demand: inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded).
    
- **Supply Curve:** Illustrates the relationship between the price of a good and the quantity producers are willing and able to sell, holding all other factors constant (Law of Supply: positive relationship between price and quantity supplied).
    

### 2. Market Equilibrium

- **Equilibrium:** Occurs at the price where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied ($Q_d = Q_s$). There is no upward or downward pressure on price.
    
- **Surplus:** When $Q_s > Q_d$, creating excess supply. Downward pressure forces the price down toward equilibrium.
    
- **Shortage:** When $Q_d > Q_s$, creating excess demand. Upward pressure bids the price up toward equilibrium.
    

### 3. Shifts vs. Movements Along Curves

- **Change in Quantity Demanded / Supplied:** Caused **only** by a change in the good's _own price_. This results in a _movement along_ the existing curve.
    
- **Change in Demand / Supply:** Caused by changes in _non-price determinants_, resulting in a _shift_ of the entire curve.
    

#### Determinants of Demand Shifts:

- **Income:** Normal goods (demand rises with income) vs. Inferior goods (demand falls with income).
    
- **Prices of Related Goods:** Substitutes (price of good A up $\rightarrow$ demand for good B up) and Complements (price of good A up $\rightarrow$ demand for good B down).
    
- **Taste and Preferences:** Favorable shifts increase demand.
    
- **Population / Number of Buyers:** Larger markets shift demand right.
    
- **Expected Future Prices:** Anticipated hikes increase current demand.
    

#### Determinants of Supply Shifts:

- **Input / Resource Costs:** Higher wages or raw material costs shift supply left.
    
- **Technology:** Technological improvements lower production costs, shifting supply right.
    
- **Prices of Related Goods (in production):** Substitutes in production.
    
- **Taxes and Subsidies:** Taxes act as a cost (shift left); subsidies reduce costs (shift right).
    
- **Number of Firms / Producers:** More market participants shift supply right.
    

## Chapter 3: Quantitative Demand Analysis

### 1. The Elasticity Concept

Elasticity measures the responsiveness of one variable to changes in another, expressed in percentage terms.

$$\text{Elasticity} = \frac{\% \text{ change in Dependent Variable}}{\% \text{ change in Independent Variable}}$$

### 2. Price Elasticity of Demand ($E_P$)

Measures the sensitivity of quantity demanded to price changes.

$$E_P = \frac{\% \Delta Q_d}{\% \Delta P} = \left( \frac{\partial Q_d}{\partial P} \right) \times \left( \frac{P}{Q} \right)$$

- **Elastic ($\vert{}E_P\vert{} > 1$):** Demand is highly responsive. A price hike _decreases_ total revenue (TR); a price cut _increases_ TR.
    
- **Inelastic ($\vert{}E_P\vert{} < 1$):** Demand is unresponsive. A price hike _increases_ total revenue; a price cut _decreases_ TR.
    
- **Unit Elastic ($\vert{}E_P\vert{} = 1$):** Total revenue is maximized; price changes do not affect total revenue.
    
- **Determinants:** Availability of close substitutes, time horizon (demand is more elastic in the long run), share of budget, and whether the good is a necessity or luxury.
    

### 3. Marginal Revenue and Price Elasticity

A crucial managerial takeaway connects price elasticity to marginal revenue ($MR$):

$$MR = P \left( 1 + \frac{1}{E_P} \right)$$

- When demand is elastic ($E_P < -1$), $MR > 0$.
    
- When demand is inelastic ($E_P > -1$), $MR < 0$.
    
- When demand is unit elastic ($E_P = -1$), $MR = 0$.
    

### 4. Other Important Elasticities

- **Cross-Price Elasticity ($E_{QX, PY}$):** Measures how the quantity demanded of good $X$ responds to a change in the price of good $Y$.
    
    - If **positive**, goods are **substitutes**.
        
    - If **negative**, goods are **complements**.
        
- **Income Elasticity ($E_Q,M$):** Measures responsiveness to changes in consumer income ($M$).
    
    - If **positive**, it is a **normal good**.
        
    - If **negative**, it is an **inferior good**.
        

### 5. Linear Demand Functions and Estimation

Managers often estimate demand using linear equations derived via regression analysis:

$$Q_x^d = \alpha_0 + \alpha_X P_X + \alpha_Y P_Y + \alpha_M M + \alpha_R R + \dots$$

- The coefficients ($\alpha$) represent marginal impacts.
    
- For instance, $\alpha_X = \frac{\partial Q_x^d}{\partial P_X}$, which is constant for a linear demand curve, meaning the _slope_ is constant, but the _price elasticity_ varies along different points of the line.

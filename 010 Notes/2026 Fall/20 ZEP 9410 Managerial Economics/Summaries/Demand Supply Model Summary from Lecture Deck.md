Here is a summary of the lecture notes on **The Demand-Supply Model**:

### **1. The Competitive Process and Market Fundamentals**

- **Competitive Markets:** A market involves buyers and sellers determining the price and quantity of goods through transactions. **Perfect competition** features many buyers and sellers, identical products, and easy entry/exit, though few real-world markets are perfectly competitive.
    
- **Demand:** The inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded (ceteris paribus). The **Law of Demand** states that as price rises, quantity demanded falls. Determinants like income, preferences, prices of related goods (substitutes/complements), and number of buyers shift the entire demand curve.
    
- **Supply:** The direct relationship between price and quantity supplied. The **Law of Supply** states that as price rises, quantity supplied increases. Determinants like technology, resource prices, input costs, and number of suppliers shift the supply curve.
    

### **2. Market Equilibrium and Disequilibrium**

- **Equilibrium:** Occurs where supply and demand balance ($Q_D = Q_S$), meaning there is no pressure to change the price. Graphically, this is the intersection of the demand and supply curves.
    
- **Disequilibrium:**
    
    - A **surplus** (excess supply) happens when the market price is above the equilibrium price, creating downward pressure on price.
        
    - A **shortage** (excess demand) happens when the market price is below the equilibrium price, creating upward pressure on price.
        
- **Comparative Statics:** Analyzing how external shocks or shifts in determinants impact equilibrium price ($P$) and quantity ($Q$). For instance, an increase in demand raises both price and quantity, while opposing shifts can result in ambiguous outcomes for either price or quantity.
    

### **3. Market Efficiency and Social Welfare**

- **Efficiency:** Achieved at the output level where marginal benefit equals marginal cost ($MB = MC$), maximizing total gains for society.
    
- **Surplus Measures:**
    
    - **Consumer Surplus (CS):** The total difference between what consumers are willing to pay (marginal benefit) and the market price they actually pay.
        
    - **Producer Surplus (PS):** The total difference between the market price received and the marginal cost of production.
        
    - **Total Surplus:** The sum of consumer surplus and producer surplus ($TS = CS + PS$), which is maximized in an unregulated competitive market.
        

### **4. Government Intervention and Policies**

- **Price Controls:**
    
    - **Price Floor:** A government-set minimum price (e.g., minimum wage) that is effective when placed above equilibrium, resulting in a surplus and deadweight loss.
        
    - **Price Ceiling:** A government-set maximum price (e.g., rent control) that is effective when placed below equilibrium, resulting in a shortage and deadweight loss.
        
- **Taxes and Subsidies:**
    
    - An **excise tax** drives a wedge between the price buyers pay and the price sellers receive, reducing quantity traded and creating a deadweight loss.
        
    - The tax burden is shared between buyers and sellers, with the less elastic (steeper curve) side bearing a greater portion of the burden.

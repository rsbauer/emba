### II. Quantitative Demand Analysis & Regression
The demand curve is modeled using multiple variables: $Q_D = \hat{a} + \hat{b}P + \hat{c}M + \hat{d}P_R$.

*   **Interpretation of Coefficients:** Each coefficient ($\hat{b}, \hat{c}, \hat{d}$) tells you the unit change in $Q_D$ when that variable changes by one unit, *holding all other variables constant* (ceteris paribus).
*   **Identifying Goods Type:**
    *   **Income ($M$):** If $\hat{c}$ is **positive**, it's a **Normal Good**. If negative, it's an inferior good.
    *   **Related Product ($P_R$):** If $\hat{d}$ is **negative**, the goods are **Complements** (e.g., cars and gas). If positive, they are substitutes.

### III. Price Elasticity of Demand (PED)
PED measures how responsive quantity demanded ($\% \Delta Q_D$) is to a change in price ($\% \Delta P$).

*   **Definition:** $E_D = \frac{\text{Percentage Change in } Q_D}{\text{Percentage Change in } P}$
    *(Note: PED is always negative due to the Law of Demand, but textbooks often ignore this sign.)*
*   **Calculation Formulas:**
    1.  **Point Formula (for a specific point):** $E_D = b \cdot \frac{P}{Q}$
    2.  **Arc Formula (approximation for large changes):** $\huge PED = \frac{\frac{Q_2-Q_1}{Q_{Avg}}}{\frac{P_2-P_1}{P_{Avg}}}$
*   **Key Interpretations (The Number Line Model):**
    | Value Range | Name | Consumer Behavior | Implication for TR |
    | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
    | $|E_D| > 1$ | **Elastic** | Consumers are highly responsive to price changes. | Price $\uparrow \implies$ TR $\downarrow$ |
    | $0 < |E_D| < 1$ | **Inelastic** | Consumers are not very responsive to price changes. | Price $\uparrow \implies$ TR $\uparrow$ |
    | $E_D = -1$ | **Unit Elastic** | % change in Q equals % change in P. | TR is maximized (at the peak). |
    | $E_D = 0$ | **Perfectly Inelastic** | Quantity demanded does not change regardless of price. | Price $\uparrow \implies$ TR $\uparrow$ |
    | $|E_D| = \infty$ | **Perfectly Elastic** | Small price increase causes demand to drop to zero. | (Rare in reality) |

### IV. PED and Total Revenue (TR)
Total Revenue is calculated as $P \times Q$. The relationship between PED and TR determines whether a price change increases or decreases total revenue.

*   **The General Rule:** $\text{% Change in Revenue} = (1 + E_D) \times \text{% Change in Price}$
*   **Core Takeaway:**
    *   If demand is **Elastic ($|E_D|>1$)**: To increase TR, the price must decrease.
    *   If demand is **Inelastic ($|E_D|<1$)**: To increase TR, the price must increase.

## Price Elasticity of Demand

Price elasticity of demand (PED) a measure of consumers' responsiveness (in terms of quantity purchased) to a price change, all other factors held constant

The elasticity coefficient of demand, $E_D$ is the ration of the percentage change in quantity demanded to the percentage change in price

These are all negative!

PED = E = percentage change in $Q_D$ / percentage change in price

Note PED is always negative (law of demand) - if the price is going up, the demand is going down. Text book ignores the negative sign. OTHER elasticities NEED the negative.

Alternative calculation using the demand equation:

$PED = b * \frac{P}{Q}$

from $Q_D = 171 - 20P + 2M - 40P_R$ 
$PED = -20 * \frac{P}{Q}$

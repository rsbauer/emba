#### General Function

q = quantity
f = function that converts input to output
L = labor, raw or low skill labor (this allows for a wage rate to be defined/used)
K = physical capital
M = raw materials

$q = f(L, K, M)$

#### Short Run
Time period where at least one input is fixed. Usually physical capital

#### Long Run
Time period long enough that no input is fixed.  The firm can vary the quantity of all inputs being utilized

Typically, **K** is the fixed input in the short run

**Average Product** is the total product per unit of input, on average
- average product of labor: $AP_L = \frac{q}{L}$
- average product of capital: $AP_K = \frac{q}{K}$

**Marginal Product** is the change in total output that results from changing input by one unit while keeping the other inputs constant
- marginal product of labor: $MP_L = \frac{\Delta q}{\Delta K}$
- marginal product of capital: $MP_K = \frac{\Delta q}{\Delta K}$

K = 1
r = $1000
w = $100
p = $2.50

determine the firm's average product of labor, marginal product of labor at each level of labor hired. What can be said about the relationship between the two measures?

at L = 3 (and Q = 120), $AP_L = MP_L$ and APL is at maximum

at L < 3 (and Q < 120)
$MP_L > AP_L$ and APL is increasing with labor

at L > 3 (and Q > 120)
$MP_L < AP_L$ and APL is decreasing with labor


> The marginals pull the averages in the direction they're going in

As labor increases while capital remains fixed, diminishing marginal returns will occur

### Costs in the Short Run

**Average total cost**

Total Cost (TC) = cost of capital (r * K) + cost of labor (wage rate, wL)

fixed cost = cost of capital (r * K)
variable cost = cost of labor (wage rate, wL)

Average total cost = total cost / quantity

Average Variable Cost = wages * labor / quantity

Marginal cost (MC) = change in total cost / quantity change

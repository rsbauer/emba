## Summary

### 🎓 Course Focus & Core Theory

**1. Strategic Communications:**
*   **Definition:** Proactively persuading stakeholders over time to maintain a favorable view of an organization or brand.
*   **Approach:** Requires an integrated approach that uses multiple platforms, blending paid and unpaid media while maintaining a consistent voice across all channels.
*   **Goal:** Messaging must be directly linked to strategic goals and objectives.

**2. Reputation Management (The Formula):**
*   Reputation is determined by the intersection of three factors: **Performance + Behavior $\times$ Communication.**
*   Communication acts as the "lever," but if performance or behavior falters, communication alone cannot fix the reputation.

**3. Crisis Communications:**
*   **Nature:** Reactive, driven by uncontrollable events (natural disasters, human error, terrorism).
*   **Goal:** Damage control and managing the narrative to help the organization return to normal operations.
*   **Crisis Protocol:** Maintain transparency by consistently communicating:
    1.  What you know.
    2.  What steps are being taken to fix it.
    3.  The status of recovery and estimated completion time.

**4. Leadership Communication (Inspiration):**
*   Great leaders inspire action by leading with **Why $\rightarrow$ How $\rightarrow$ What** (the purpose before the plan), rather than defaulting to a linear "What $\rightarrow$ How $\rightarrow$ Why."

### 📝 Major Assignments & Deliverables

**1. Leadership Communication Analysis (Due Sept 26):**
*   Students must analyze the communication styles of a chosen leader (living, dead, or fictional).
*   The presentation requires background context and detailed analysis of their specific communication style.

**2. Midterm Group Presentation (Crisis Simulation):**
*   **Scenario:** A group case study involving a major crisis (e.g., a healthcare network hack).
*   **Role:** Students act as communication consultants, advising the CEO on response strategies (e.g., paying ransom, maintaining services).
*   **Focus:** The quality of the communications strategy is paramount; it must be flexible and address all stakeholders.

**3. Presentation Skills:**
*   Students are expected to practice public speaking skills (Toastmasters recommended) and keep presentations concise (avoiding sentences over 25 words).

# Strategic Communications Overview

## Reputation: The Big Three

Reputation = Performance + Behavior * Communication

Communications is the lever, but if performance or behavior is off, communication will not help.

- Proactive
	- Seeks to proactively persuade stakeholders to have a favorable view of your organization, brand, or service over time
	- 80% of Bloomberg is a pitched story (the rest is breaking news)
	- Messaging is linked directly to strategic goals, objectives (if isn't, then there's a problem!)
	- Integrated approach
		- Uses multiple platforms
		- Blends paid media with unpaid (advertising, commercial, and social media)
		- Speaks across channels with consistent voice, messages
		- Seeks to build dialogues with key stakeholders
	- Measurable
- Reactive
	- Responding to routine media inquiries
	- Response to a crisis
		- Damage control
		- Seek to manage narrative
			- Tell your side of the story
		- Help business/organization recover and return to normal operations
	- Driven by uncontrollables
		- Human error (BP Gulf Oil Crisis, Exxon-Valdez)
		- Terrorist activities (9/11)
		- Catastrophes (plane crashes, explosions)
		- Natural disasters (earthquakes, floods, hurricanes, tornadoes)
		- Behavioral issues
	- Only as good as management of the crisis
- Crisis
	- "It takes 20 years to build a reputation and 5 minutes to ruin it.  If you think about that, you'll do things differently", Warren Buffet

## Summary
### 1. Market Structure & Competition
*   **Market:** The process where buyers and sellers interact to set the price and quantity of goods/services.
*   **Perfect Competition:** A theoretical ideal characterized by many buyers, many sellers, identical products, no barriers to entry, and easy exit (few real-world markets meet these criteria).

### 2. Demand
*   **Definition:** The inverse relationship between a product's price and the quantity consumers are willing and able to buy (**Law of Demand**). As price rises, quantity demanded falls, *ceteris paribus*.
*   **Movement vs. Shift:** A change in **price** causes a movement *along* the demand curve (change in quantity demanded). Changes in external factors cause the entire curve to **shift**.
*   **Determinants of Demand (Shifters):**
    *   Consumer Preferences/Advertising ($\uparrow$ increases demand)
    *   Income (Normal goods: $\uparrow$ income $\implies \uparrow$ demand; Inferior goods: $\uparrow$ income $\implies \downarrow$ demand)
    *   Price of Related Goods (Substitutes: $\uparrow$ price of substitute $\implies \uparrow$ demand for your product)
    *   Number of Buyers ($\uparrow$ number of buyers $\implies \uparrow$ demand)

### 3. Supply
*   **Definition:** The relationship between a product's price and the quantity producers are willing and able to offer (**Law of Supply**). As price rises, quantity supplied rises.
*   **Movement vs. Shift:** A change in **price** causes a movement *along* the supply curve (change in quantity supplied). Changes in external factors cause the entire curve to **shift**.
*   **Determinants of Supply (Shifters):**
    *   Technology ($\uparrow$ technology $\implies \uparrow$ supply)
    *   Price of Related Goods (Substitutes in production: $\uparrow$ price of substitute $\implies \downarrow$ supply of your product)

### 4. Market Equilibrium & Disequilibrium
*   **Equilibrium:** The point where the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied ($Q_d = Q_s$). At this point, market forces balance, and there is no pressure for the price to change.
*   **Disequilibrium:**
    *   **Surplus (Excess Supply):** Occurs when the market price is **above** the equilibrium price ($Q_s > Q_d$). Market forces push prices down toward equilibrium.
    *   **Shortage (Excess Demand):** Occurs when the market price is **below** the equilibrium price ($Q_d > Q_s$). Market forces push prices up toward equilibrium.

### 5. Comparative Statics & Efficiency
*   **Comparative Statics:** Analyzing how changes in one variable (e.g., increased demand) affect other variables (equilibrium price and quantity).
    *   *Example:* $\uparrow$ Demand $\implies P^e \uparrow$ and $Q^e \uparrow$.
*   **Market Efficiency:** The market outcome at equilibrium is considered efficient because it maximizes **Total Surplus**.
*   **Total Surplus:** This is the sum of **Consumer Surplus (CS)** (the benefit buyers receive) and **Producer Surplus (PS)** (the profit sellers earn). At equilibrium, this total surplus is maximized.

### Perfect Competition
- many buyers and many sellers
- each buyer and/or seller has a negligible impact on the market outcome
- identical product (good or service)
- no barriers to entry - any one can enter
- easy entry and exit

Few markets (if any) are perfectly competitive markets

### Demand

**Demand** - the inverse relationship between the price of a product and the quantity consumers are willing and able to purchase during a given time period (quantity demanded), ceteris paribus (all other things held constant)

**Law of demand** - ceteris paribus (all things constant), an increase in the price of a good leads to a decrease in the quantity demanded, and vice versa.

- Buyers willing to purchase at higher prices have a higher valuation on the product
- Less buyers are willing to purchase at higher prices compared to lower prices

### Supply

**Supply** - the relationship between the price of a product and the quantity producers are willing and able to offer during a given time period (quantity supplied), ceteris paribus (all other things held constant)

**Law of supply** - ceteris paribus (all things constant), an increase in the price of a good leads to an increase in the quantity supplied, and vice versa

- sellers willing to sell at higher prices have a higher cost associated with "producing" the good or service
- More sellers are willing to sell at higher prices compared to lower prices

### Market Disequilibrium

If the market is in disequilibrium:

- The current market price occurs at a price where the quantity supplied is not equal to the quantity demanded  
- market surplus (excess quantity supplied) occurs when market price > equilibrium price  
- market shortage (excess quantity demanded) occurs when market price < equilibrium price
- market forces guide the toward the equilibrium price

Market will work towards equilibrium if left alone (but may take time).  Government intervention is the only thing that can disrupt this.

### Comparative Statics
- demand increases - $P^e$ increases and $Q^e$ increases
- demand decreases - $P^e$ decreases and $Q^e$ decreases

- supply increases - $P^e$ decreases and $Q^e$ increases
- supply decreases - $P^e$ increases and $Q^e$ decreases

- demand and supply increases
	- $P^e$ change is ambiguous and $Q^e$ increases
- demand decreases and supply increases
	- $P^e$ decreases and $Q^e$ change is ambiguous

## Market Efficiency

**Efficiency** - maximization of output through careful allocation of resources, given the constraints of demand (buyers' preferences) and producers' costs

Market Efficiency
- only those points on or below the demand curve are acceptable to buyers | points on demand curve reveal willingness to pay
- only those points above or on the supply curve are acceptable to sellers (producers) | points on the supply curve reveal willingness to supply


Market efficiency = producing what consumers want and the value to the person of the lowest evaluation and is equal to the person with the highest cost

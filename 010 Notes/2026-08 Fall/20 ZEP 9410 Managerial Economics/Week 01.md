### 🌍 Core Economic Concepts
*   **The Economic Problem:** The fundamental challenge that society faces because **resources (labor, capital, raw materials) are scarce** while human wants are unlimited.
*   **Economics:** The study of how societies manage these scarce resources by answering three questions: *What* to produce? *How* to produce it? And *For whom*?
*   **Managerial Economics:** Applies microeconomic theories and analytical tools specifically to help businesses make optimal decisions regarding resource management.
*   **Market Economy:** Resources are allocated through the combined, voluntary choices of households (consumers) and firms (sellers).

### ⚙️ Decision-Making Tools & Principles
Effective decision-making relies on several core principles:
1.  **Opportunity Cost:** The value of the *next best alternative* that must be foregone when making a choice.
2.  **Tradeoffs:** Recognizing that choosing one thing means giving up another.
3.  **Marginal Analysis:** Decisions should be made by comparing the **Marginal Benefit (MB)** from an action to its **Marginal Cost (MC)**. *(Rule: If MB > MC, take the action.)*
4.  **Incentives:** People and firms respond predictably to incentives (rewards or penalties).

### 💰 Firms, Profit, and Costs
*   **Firms:** Organizations that convert inputs (labor, capital, materials) into outputs (goods/services). They can be for-profit, nonprofit, NGO, or government agencies.
*   **Goal of For-Profit Firms:** To maximize profit ($\text{Profit} = \text{Total Revenue} - \text{Total Costs}$).
*   **Cost Types:**
    *   **Explicit Cost:** Monetary payments (e.g., wages, rent).
    *   **Implicit Cost:** Foregone opportunity (e.g., the interest you could have earned on your own capital).
    *   **Opportunity Cost (True Cost):** The sum of all explicit and implicit costs; this is the relevant cost for business decisions.
*   **Profit Types:**
    *   **Accounting Profit:** $\text{Total Revenue} - \text{Explicit Costs}$ (Overstates true profit because it ignores opportunity cost).
    *   **Economic Profit/Loss:** $\text{Total Revenue} - \text{Opportunity Cost}$. This is the measure that signals whether a firm should continue operating.

### 📈 Markets and Demand
*   **Market:** A group of buyers and sellers who determine the price and quantity of goods or services through competition.
*   **Demand:** The **Law of Demand** states there is an inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded: *As price increases, the quantity consumers are willing to buy decreases (ceteris paribus).*
*   **Perfect Competition:** A market structure characterized by many buyers and sellers, identical products, no barriers to entry, and negligible impact from any single participant.

### 💡 Effective Management Principles
An effective manager must:
1.  Identify goals and constraints.
2.  Understand both the markets and the incentives at play.
3.  Use data-driven decision-making.
4.  Apply marginal analysis to optimize outcomes.

# For-Profit Firms
The economic goal of these firms is to maximize profits

***Profit = total revenue - total costs***

**Total Revenue** - the firm's total receipts from the sale of its output

**Total Revenue** = price * quantity

**Total Costs** - the firms ...

**Explicit cost** - monetary expenditure
**Implicit cost** - foregone opportunity. No monetary payment

Implicit cost example could be $1 million dollars spent to start a company and buy equipment, you lose out on interest you could have gotten from things like bonds and such.

Depreciation of equipment would also be an implicit cost (the $1 million depreciated to $900,000 then $100,000 is implicit cost)

**Sunk cost** - an unchangeable past cost. Not relevant to current business decisions 

**Accounting cost** - explicit costs and value of depreciation of assets

**Opportunity costs** - return from the best alternative use of resources

Explicit and implicit costs are relevant to business decisions
***total costs = opportunity costs = explicit costs + implicit costs***

### Accounting Profit
profits based on explicit costs
accounting profit = total revenue - accounting costs

Econimic profit or loss
profit based on the opportunity cost of producing
economic profit = total revenue - opportunity cost

If economic profit < 0, the firm should consider not entering the business or evaluate its production options if it is currently in operation

## Marginal Analysis

**Marginal analysis** compares the marginal benefit (MB) from an action to its marginal cost (MC), and uses this comparison to decide whether to take the action

Marginal Analysis Rule
If MB > MC, then the action should be taken (performed)

Using the marginal analysis rule 
- firm's goal - to maximize profit from selling its good or service
- firm will sell its output as long as MB > MC for the last unit

# Demand

**Demand** - the inverse relationship between the price of a product and the quantity consumers are willing and able to purchase during a given time period (quantity demanded), ceteris paribus (all other things held constant)

**Law of demand** ceteris paribus (all things constant!), an increase in the price of a good leads to decrease in the quantity demanded, and vice versa.

- Buyers are willing to purchase at higher prices have a higher valuation on the product
- Less buyers are willing to purchase at higher prices compared to lower prices

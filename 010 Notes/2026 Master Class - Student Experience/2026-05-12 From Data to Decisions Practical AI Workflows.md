## 01 Business Case for AI Workflows

Discussed workflows 

### The Executive’s Dilemma
5.5% orgs generate real bottom-line impact from AI - despite 88% claiming to use it

3x more likely to have fundamentally redesigned workflows - the #1 predictor separte winners from the rest

Data is everywhere.  Insight is scarce.

Orgs generate more data in a data than analysts can process in a month.

Bottleneck is no longer data, it’s decision velocity

Companies that build AI into their decision workflow are now 18-24 months ahead of those that don’t

The skill gap is real: leaders who can direct AI are already pulling away from those who can’t


### Using AI as a tool (no!)
- Ask a one-off question
- Get an answer, move on
- No repeatable structure
- Quality depends on luck of prompt

### Using AI as a workflow (yes!)
- Structured sequence of human + AI steps
- Consistent inputs -> consistent quality
- Teachable to your team
- Gets better with iteration

There are multiple levels of AI use - people who can USE AI and people who can LEVERAGE AI

Workflow = a repeatable sequence of human + AI steps that produces a better decision, faster

Are we using data to predict or forecast?  Prediction is ok, but forecast the data matters

Is it ok to have AI unsupervised?  maybe.


## 02 The Four Questions Framework

Descriptive, Diagnostic, Predictive, Prescriptive
#### Four types of questions every leader should ask of data

### Descriptive: What happened?
Patterns of spending and outcomes across strategies

### Diagnostic: Why did it happen?
Difference in outcomes between different strategies

### Predictive: What will happen?
If I allocate $6,000 across channels, how many clicks will I get?

### Prescriptive: How can I make it happen?
At what budget does marginal return flatten - the CFO’s inflection point?

These questions are from Gartner Analytics Ascendancy Model
Value vs difficulty: Descriptive, Diagnostic, Predictive, Prescriptive

AI vs HI: 
Descriptive is mostly AI
Diagnostic: almost 50% AI vs HI
Predictive: mostly HI
Prescriptive: almost all HI

### Prompt craft is a leadership skill

Weak prompt: “How should I allocate my marketing budget?”

Strong prompt: “I have $6,000. Allocate it across these 20 campaigns. Maximize cliks.  Do not exceed channel caps.”

1. Set context first
2. State the objective
3. Name the constraints
4. Iterate deliberately
